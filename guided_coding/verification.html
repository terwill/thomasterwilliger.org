What the evidence says about /gc as now written

/gc version 1.4 keeps the steps the replay study found to help, drops or makes optional those that showed no clear benefit, narrows the one that probably hurt (estimated, not tested), and keeps a few safety steps that were not measured. Figures are major errors left in the code per run on 18 harder jobs (jobs where the earlier kit's own checks had caught errors, not a random sample), unless stated otherwise; the study is described below.

Two scorings of the same arms. Plain Claude Code with one review appears as 0.79 (round 6) and as 0.85 (before the outside review, round 6b); /gc 1.3 appears as 0.97 and 1.00. Round 6b's instruments found further errors that the earlier versions also had, which raised those figures. Each comparison uses figures from one scoring.

Kept for safety and clarity, not measured: the short final report (what changed, what was checked, what is open); a real call to a live service where a fix depends on it; exact commands for another platform; an outside review for changes that touch existing user files or credentials; asking before any commit, push or installation; a plan, a checked recovery step and an outside review before an irreversible step in consequential work; /gc batch; the review fallback when the code-review skill is missing.

Not known: /gc 1.4 has not itself been replayed (it was checked in a small trial, below), and the narrower scope rule was adopted without a test. Also not known: whether /gc 1.4 as a whole leaves fewer errors than plain Claude Code with one review and an outside review; how much of the developer's own time an outside review takes; and whether an outside review helps on routine fixes (it is optional there). The effects found are small (about 0.1 to 0.2 major errors per run) next to the variation between runs.

The replay study (October 2026)

37 past bug-fix jobs in PHENIX and cctbx, done with the earlier GuidedCoding kit, were replayed with plain Claude Code: alone (P), and with one review pass using Claude Code's code-review skill (P+R). Every result was scored for program errors left, with errors tagged major or minor without knowing which method produced them.

per job, over all 37 jobsearlier kitPP+R
program errors left (missed)0.510.810.47
major errors left (missed)0.160.330.24

What it shows:

  • One review pass after plain Claude Code made a large difference, at about half the plain run's time.
  • The earlier kit and P+R were level on all errors.
  • The earlier kit led on major errors (0.16 against 0.24). Over all 37 jobs that lead rests on one job; without it, the two are level (0.167 against 0.160).
  • A deeper search of the 18 jobs where the kit's layers caught something told a different story. On major errors left in the code (missed plus disclosed), the kit's lead there was 0.19 per run, leaving out the same job (0.35 against 0.54). Part of that increase is an artefact of the deeper search, which started from errors in the plain runs. On the round-4 measure (missed only) the lead in those jobs was 0.05. This is unresolved, and is the main evidence against the change to this procedure.
  • The earlier kit's outside reviewers and live checks made some major catches that no in-session step recovered. This procedure keeps outside review and live checks, as steps triggered by the kind of change.
  • A GC-style second reviewer, run after the review pass on the 18 harder jobs, left fewer major errors than the review pass alone, but not clearly fewer than a second ordinary review, and took about three times as long. It is offered here as an optional step (REVIEWER.md).

What it does not show:

  • This study's 37 jobs were not replayed with this procedure; round 6 (below) replayed it on 18 of them. Its added steps (live calls, platform commands, the optional reviewer) were not measured as built into it.
  • 37 jobs is a small sample, from one developer's work. Every worker, scorer and checker was a Claude model; some outside readers were not.
  • The study measured errors in code. It did not measure accidental damage to installations. For that kind of work this procedure requires a plan, a checked recovery step and a completed outside review.

The replay of /gc itself (round 6, October 2026)

/gc version 1.3 was replayed on the 18 harder jobs, those where the earlier kit's layers had caught something: 38 fresh runs, with the same Claude Code versions, inputs and sandbox as the plain runs, and a fixed paragraph standing in for the developer ("keep to the task"). In 4 runs /gc wrote an outside review; it was sent to a model from another family and the session continued with the reply. Every run was scored at the same depth as the others, and the reading rule was fixed before any result was seen.

major errors left in the code, per run (missed or disclosed)earlier kitPP+R/gc with its outside reviews
the 18 harder jobs0.440.900.790.90
  • The result: no clear difference between /gc and plain Claude Code with one review pass. The estimate was slightly worse for /gc (+0.11 major errors per run; 90% interval −0.19 to +0.42). It does not show that the two are equal, and /gc did not recover the earlier kit's lead on these jobs.
  • Questions for the developer. /gc disclosed more of what it left, often as a question for the developer, which the replay's fixed stand-in always answered "keep to the task". Had those questions been answered as /gc recommended, the two would have been about level (an untested estimate).
  • The outside review, which /gc wrote on its own trigger in 4 of 38 runs, removed 3 major errors.
  • What followed. As decided before the results were seen, /gc was kept as a way of working with the developer, not as a claim that it produces more correct code than plain Claude Code with a review. Round 6b (below) then measured the outside review, and version 1.4 made it standard for hard changes and narrowed the scope rule.

The round-6 report is part of the final report above (Part F).

What an outside review adds (round 6b, October 2026)

Every counted run of round 6, both /gc and plain Claude Code with one review, was resumed once with an outside review of its final change by a model from another family (gpt-6.1-sol), built by one script for both, and scored again at the same depth. The reading rule was fixed before any run.

major errors left in the code, per runbefore the outside reviewafter
plain Claude Code + one review (38 runs)0.850.76
/gc 1.3 (38 runs)1.000.83
  • For /gc: the outside review "adds something" by the rule fixed in advance: −0.17 (90% interval −0.31 to −0.06), also without either of the two jobs that most often drive results. The margin is thin: 9 run-level removals of a major error and 3 additions, a net of 6 removals, all of the net in 4 jobs (one more job had 2 of each), and a single changed ruling (for example one error's severity) would turn it into "no clear difference".
  • For plain Claude Code with one review: no clear difference (−0.08), as a check made before the runs predicted: the reviews named few of the errors that runs had left.
  • After the review, /gc was still not better than plain Claude Code with one review and the same outside review (+0.07 major errors per run, not a clear difference).
  • What followed: the outside review became a standard step for hard and consequential changes in /gc 1.4, optional for other jobs. The study did not test the scope rule separately; it was narrowed on the evidence above rather than by a further experiment.

The round-6b report is part of the final report above (Part G).

Use since

The procedure, as an earlier version, was used on three real jobs on 2026-10-23, and the developer judged it worked well. Evaluation continues on 10 to 15 jobs, each checked for later defects about four weeks after the job.

Trial of version 1.4 (2026-10-26)

An implementation check, not a measurement. Fresh /gc 1.4 runs on replayed jobs:

runresult
three hard jobs (a consistency check, process polling, a model-default update)trigger H applied in each, with a one-line reason; each wrote an outside review file with the task, why the change is hard, the full diff, and the tests on the old and new code
a routine one-line fixno trigger: "one clear defect on one path"; the outside review was offered, not required
the three hard jobs, after the outside review came backevery finding was handled (1, 3 and 2 findings), each fix with a test; each report opened with "Ready to use: no" and named the remaining platform or live check with its commands

One fix in the trial was narrower than it might have been: on the model-default job, the no-temperature handling was given to the two new default models only, and the report presented this as a design choice rather than an open point.

What the first runs changed: a session must finish (or stop) any long test before writing the review file and the report, and leave nothing running; and the narrowed scope rule replaced the strict one before the second runs.

What these runs changed:

  • the auto guard became a whole-word match, and the retired-command answer says that /gc batch is supervised;
  • the review-fix scope rule named what counts as a behaviour change (in 1.3; 1.4 replaced it, see the table above);
  • batch patches now include new and staged files, and are checked before reverting;
  • the batch report checks whether accepted patches apply together.
